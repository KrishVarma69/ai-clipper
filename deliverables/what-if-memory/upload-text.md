## Fact notes (the story is fiction; these are the real claims in it)
| Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| A normal person forgets more than half of something new within a day; measured in 1885 | Hermann Ebbinghaus, *Über das Gedächtnis* (1885); replicated by Murre & Dros (2015) | ✅ His savings data fell to about a third after one day. The on-screen curve is illustrative |
| Most people can hold about seven digits at once | George Miller, "The Magical Number Seven" (1956); typical digit span | ✅ |
| Top memory athletes memorise a shuffled deck in under 20 seconds | World Memory Championships records (about 12–13 s) | ✅ |
| The average person knows about 5,000 faces | Jenkins, Dowsett & Burton, *Proc. R. Soc. B* (2018), University of York | ✅ |
| Sleep replays the day and decides what to keep | Hippocampal replay and sleep-dependent memory consolidation | ✅ Simplified |
| Core body temperature below 35°C is hypothermia; older people are more vulnerable | Standard medical definition (NHS, CDC) | ✅ |
| 2006: UC Irvine researchers described Jill Price, who recalls almost any day of her adult life | Parker, Cahill & McGaugh, *Neurocase* (2006) | ✅ |
| Called highly superior autobiographical memory; very few people have it | LePort et al. (2012) | ✅ |
| A 2013 study found people with HSAM still form false memories | Patihis et al., *PNAS* (2013) | ✅ |
| Jill Price has called her memory a burden, not a gift | Her own descriptions (letter quoted in Parker et al., 2006; memoir *The Woman Who Can't Forget*, 2008) | ✅ Paraphrased |
