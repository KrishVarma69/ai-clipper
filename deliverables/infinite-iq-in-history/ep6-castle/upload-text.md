The real history behind the story:
• Château Gaillard, in Normandy, was built by Richard the Lionheart in about two years (1196–1198). He reportedly called it his "beautiful castle on the rock". Its ruins still stand.
• King Philip II of France besieged it from 1203 to March 1204, roughly seven months.
• French miners brought down part of the outer defences. According to the chronicler William the Breton, soldiers then got into the middle bailey through an unguarded chapel window near the latrines. The story is often retold as climbing up the toilet chute.
• To save food, the castle's commander, Roger de Lacy, sent hundreds of townspeople out of the castle. The French refused to let many through, and they were trapped between the lines through the winter until Philip allowed the survivors to leave.
• Counterweight trebuchets used a heavy falling box to fling a light arm and sling, throwing heavy stones hundreds of metres.
• Besiegers dug mines under walls and burned the wooden props to collapse them; defenders listened for digging with simple detectors.
Hugo, Guy, Maud and the story are made up. Emergency tips: ration early, boil water if unsure, and check the windows, not just the front door.
