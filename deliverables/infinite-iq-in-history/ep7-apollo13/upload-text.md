The real history behind the story:
• Apollo 13 launched on 11 April 1970 with Jim Lovell, Jack Swigert and Fred Haise, planned as the third Moon landing.
• About 56 hours into the flight, on 13 April, oxygen tank 2 exploded when the crew stirred the tanks. NASA's review board found that wiring inside the tank had been damaged during ground testing before launch.
• "Houston, we've had a problem" (Swigert said it first; Lovell repeated it).
• The crew used the lunar module Aquarius as a lifeboat. It was designed to support two people for about two days and had to support three for about four.
• The command module's square lithium-hydroxide canisters didn't fit the lunar module's round sockets; engineers improvised an adapter from cardboard, plastic bags, suit hoses and duct tape, nicknamed "the mailbox", and read the instructions up to the crew.
• With the guidance computer powered down, Lovell steered a course-correction burn of about 14 seconds by keeping the Earth's day–night line steady in the window.
• Astronaut Ken Mattingly, removed from the crew days before launch after measles exposure, helped work out the power-up procedures in a simulator.
• Re-entry radio blackout lasted around six minutes, longer than usual. The capsule splashed down safely in the Pacific on 17 April 1970.
