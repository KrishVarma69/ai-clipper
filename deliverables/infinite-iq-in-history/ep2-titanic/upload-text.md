The real history behind the story:
• Titanic struck the iceberg at about 11:40 pm on 14 April 1912 and sank at about 2:20 am, roughly 2 hours 40 minutes later. About 2,224 people were aboard and more than 1,500 died.
• Its 20 lifeboats held 1,178 people, about half of those aboard, but that was more than the outdated British Board of Trade rules required. Lifeboat 7, the first launched, could carry 65 and left with about 28.
• The lookouts had no binoculars: the key to the locker went home with Second Officer David Blair, who was removed from the crew before the voyage.
• Wireless operators sent passengers' private messages while ice warnings came in from several ships that day (six are commonly cited).
• Titanic had 16 watertight compartments and could float with four flooded. The iceberg opened five or six.
• Gates and barriers did separate third class, as US immigration rules required. How much they held people back that night is still debated.
• The Californian was close enough to see Titanic's rockets, but its wireless operator had gone to bed. The Carpathia raced about 58 miles through ice and rescued about 705 people.
• The sea was about −2 °C. Salt water stays liquid below 0 °C.
Pip, Reggie, Nora, Finn and the story are made up. Safety tips: in cold water, float for the first minute while the shock passes; always wear the life jacket; find your muster point before you need it.
