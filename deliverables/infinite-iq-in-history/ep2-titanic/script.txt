You have infinite IQ. And you just woke up on the Titanic. April tenth, nineteen twelve. Third class. A bunk the size of a coffin, and a man snoring above you like a foghorn. And you know exactly how this trip ends. Meet Pip. Fourteen years old. Bellboy. Best ship ever built, this. Unsinkable! He says it every five minutes. In four days, you'll build a telescope out of a pair of spectacles. You'll get locked below deck by the richest boy on the ship. And at twenty to midnight on the fourteenth of April... the ocean will ask you one question. So what if you had infinite IQ on the Titanic?
First, the facts in your head. On the night of April fourteenth, the Titanic hits an iceberg. Two hours and forty minutes later, it's gone. More than two thousand two hundred people on board. Lifeboat seats... for about half of them. Pip, how many lifeboats are there? Twenty! More than the law says! The law was the problem. It was written for much smaller ships.
You try to warn people. How does that go? About as well as you'd expect. Then you meet Reggie Vane. Seventeen. First class. His father owns a bank and two railways... and every steward on this ship jumps when Reggie snaps his fingers. And there are gates. Between third class and the upper decks. Mind the gate, third class. Some of us paid for the view.
Day two. You climb up to the lookouts. Two men, freezing, high on the mast, staring into the dark. With what? No binoculars. The key to the binocular locker? It went home with an officer who left the ship before it sailed. That's real. So... you make a telescope. A lens from a reading glass at the front. A lens from a short-sighted passenger's spectacles at the back. How do you get them? You trade a coin trick. Line them up in a tube, the right distance apart, and far things look close. Galileo did the same trick three hundred years ago. Pip looks through it. That seagull's got a face! All seagulls have faces. Not like that one.
Day three. The radio room. Two operators, tapping Morse code all day... for rich passengers. Arriving Tuesday. Bring the dog. And between the dog messages, warnings. Ice ahead. Ice ahead. Large icebergs. Six warnings, from six different ships. You learn Morse code in one afternoon. Dot dot dot. Dash dash dash. Dot dot dot. S, O, S. And upstairs? Nobody wants to hear it... from third class.
So Reggie makes it official. One word to the right steward, and you're locked below. The very bottom of the ship. Right at the front. Where the iceberg will hit. This is the worst cabin on the whole ship. It's perfect. Because down here, you find what nobody upstairs ever looks at. The blueprints. The Titanic is split into sixteen watertight compartments. It can float with four of them flooded. Five, and it sinks. And the iceberg? It opens five. Maybe six.
Then you meet Nora. Twelve. Third class. Travelling to her aunt in New York, with her little brother Finn. Finn is six and has decided you're a wizard. Do another one. Do the coin one. So you do the coin one. Every time. Nora doesn't laugh. She just asks you, very quietly... Is something bad going to happen? You don't lie to her. You teach her a knot instead. A bowline. The knot that doesn't slip. If you need to tie anything together... use this one.
Day four. April fourteenth. The coldest night of the trip. The sea is flat as glass. No moon. No waves breaking around the base of icebergs to make them easy to see. At twenty to midnight, a lookout spots it. Through your telescope, a little earlier than history says. Does it matter? The Titanic weighs more than forty thousand tons. At twenty two knots, turning it is like turning a city. Thirty seven seconds later... a long scraping shudder along the side. You hear it from the bottom of the ship. Then you hear the water.
You do the maths before anyone else. Five compartments open. About two hours. So who do you need? The boy the stewards obey. Reggie! The gates! He's standing in the corridor in his silk pyjamas. Lifeboats are for first class. That's how it works. The first lifeboats are leaving half empty. Boat seven has room for sixty five people. It's lowering with about twenty eight. Every empty seat is a person. Reggie looks at the gate. Then at the steward holding the keys. Will he say it?
Meanwhile, you need more seats. The deck is covered in wooden deck chairs. Wood floats. So does cork. And Nora knows a knot that doesn't slip. You, Nora and Pip lash deck chairs into rafts, bowline after bowline. Then you see it... lights. Another ship, maybe ten miles away. Close enough to see. Too far to hear. Their radio operator went to bed at half past eleven. So you grab the signal lamp. Dot dot dot. Dash dash dash. Dot dot dot. Over and over, into the dark.
One forty five. Water on the forward deck. The last boats. The air is so cold it hurts to breathe. The sea is two degrees below zero. Salt water can drop below freezing... and stay liquid. In water like that, most people have fifteen to thirty minutes. There's one seat left in the last boat. Reggie has a ticket for it. And standing next to him... Finn. Six years old. Holding Nora's hand. The seat... or the boy.
Reggie looks at the seat... then he lifts Finn into the boat. And then he runs below, deck after deck, ordering every steward to open every gate in third class... until there are no gates left. Pip shouts it across the deck. Unsinkable, Reggie! You're unsinkable!
Two twenty. The Titanic slips under. The rafts hold. The bowlines hold. At four in the morning, a ship called the Carpathia arrives. It raced fifty eight miles through the ice, in the dark, after hearing the S O S. Nora is in a lifeboat. Finn is asleep on her lap. And on a raft made of deck chairs... Reggie Vane, soaked, freezing... and alive.
So, is any of this real? Sadly, most of it. The Titanic really sank in two hours and forty minutes. More than fifteen hundred people died. Its twenty lifeboats had room for only about half the people on board, and many left the ship far from full. The binocular key really was left behind. The Carpathia really raced to the rescue and saved about seven hundred people. And another ship, the Californian, was close enough to see the Titanic's rockets... but its radio was switched off for the night. Nora, Finn, Pip and Reggie are made up for the story. The real people on that ship were not.
Three things, in case it's ever you. If you fall into cold water, don't swim and don't panic. Lie back, and float for one minute while the shock passes. Always wear the life jacket, even if you can swim. And on any boat, find your muster point before you need it. Even geniuses forget.
So here's the question. Day three. Reggie offers you a first class cabin, if you stop talking about icebergs. Or you stay at the bottom of the ship, with the blueprints. Comment DECK or DEEP. And subscribe, because next time... you have infinite IQ in the Black Death.
