You have infinite IQ. And you just woke up in Sicily. October, thirteen forty seven. The port of Messina. You're lying in a pile of fishing nets... and something is wriggling in your hood. Fresh fish! Mostly fresh! This is Tommaso. Thirteen. He sells fish, and he has never once smelled good. In the next few weeks, you'll build a flea trap out of a candle and a bowl of soapy water. You'll get thrown out of the city by the doctor's son. And when the sickness reaches his own house... he'll have to choose between his father's book and you. So what if you had infinite IQ in the Black Death?
First, look at the harbour. Twelve ships just sailed in from the east. And on some of them, the sailors are dying at the oars. You know exactly what this is. The Black Death. In the next five years, it will kill about one in every three people in Europe. Maybe more. What causes it? A germ. A bacterium so small that nobody will identify it for more than five hundred years. It lives in fleas. The fleas live on rats. And the rats live on ships.
The problem? Nobody here knows germs exist. The best doctors in Europe blame bad air... or the planets. Meet Lorenzo. Sixteen. His father is the city's physician, Master Galeno. Lorenzo carries a little bag of flowers to sniff, so the bad air can't get him. My father studied in Bologna. You studied fish.
You try to warn people. Who believes a boy who sleeps in fishing nets? Kill the fleas? Next you'll tell us to wash. So you start anyway.
Day two. Fleas jump towards warmth and light. So... you make a trap. A bowl of water, a drop of soap, and a candle in the middle. The flea jumps at the flame, lands in the water... and the soap breaks the surface, so it can't climb out. By morning, Tommaso counts them. Forty one! I'm naming this one Lorenzo. These tiny things are the real killers. Not the air. Not the stars.
Day three. The rats. They don't swim to shore. They walk... down the mooring ropes. So you nail old barrel lids around the ropes, like collars. A rat can't climb past a disc it can't hold on to. Sailors still do this today. Rat guards. Tommaso laughs. Rats with hats! And Lorenzo laughs too... then tells his father.
Master Galeno makes it official. The fish boy is spreading fear. He leaves the city. Tonight. So you walk out through the gate, up the hill, to an empty farm above the town. This is the worst place in Sicily. No walls. No food. Goats. It's perfect. Because when a sickness spreads from person to person... the safest place to be is far away from people.
Day five. Someone climbs the hill with bread. Bianca. Twelve. Lorenzo's little sister. She has read every book in her father's house... twice. Girls aren't allowed to be doctors. So she asks you questions instead. Why does the trap work? Why the soap? Why up here? So you teach her. Wash your hands. Boil the clothes. Burn the bedding. Keep the sick apart from the healthy... for forty days.
Forty days? Why forty? Because it's long enough to be sure. Thirty years from now, the city of Ragusa will make exactly this rule for every ship that arrives. Later, the Italians will call it quaranta giorni. Forty days. You know it as quarantine.
Day nine. The city finally throws the ships out of the harbour. Too late. The rats are already ashore. The sickness spreads street by street. Fever. Swelling under the arms. Master Galeno treats them all, with the best medicine he knows. He bleeds them. He tells them to shut the windows. He sniffs his flowers. And then... Master Galeno gets sick.
At midnight, someone is shouting at the farm gate. Lorenzo. He's carrying Bianca. She's burning with fever. His father's rule says the sick stay locked inside the house. Your rule says the sick come up the hill, away from everyone else. If he takes her back, his father will never forgive him. If he stays... he's trusting the fish boy. The book... or his sister.
Lorenzo looks back at the city. Then he carries her through the gate.
Now what? There are no antibiotics. Penicillin won't be discovered until nineteen twenty eight. So you do what you can. Clean water, boiled first. A pinch of salt and a spoon of honey in every cup, so her body doesn't dry out. Fresh straw. Fresh air. Flea traps around the bed. Tommaso keeps the candles lit all night. Lorenzo doesn't sleep at all.
On the fourth morning, Bianca opens her eyes... and asks why the trap works.
By winter, the farm on the hill has forty beds. Lorenzo runs it. Bianca writes everything down. And Master Galeno? He survives. He climbs the hill himself and reads Bianca's notes... twice. And Tommaso? Fresh fish! Mostly fresh! Some things never change.
So, is any of this real? The Black Death really arrived in Messina in October thirteen forty seven, on ships from the east. The city did throw the ships out... too late. Between thirteen forty seven and thirteen fifty one, it killed something like a third to half of Europe. Scientists have found the plague germ's DNA in the teeth of victims. Rats and their fleas spread it, though many scientists now think human fleas and lice helped too. And Ragusa really did invent the quarantine, in thirteen seventy seven. The famous beaked plague doctor masks came three hundred years later. Tommaso, Lorenzo, Bianca and Galeno are made up for the story.
Three things, in case it's ever you. Wash your hands with soap for twenty seconds. Soap breaks many germs apart, and washes the rest away. If you're sick, stay home and keep your distance. And plague still exists today... but antibiotics cure it, if you see a doctor early. Even geniuses forget.
