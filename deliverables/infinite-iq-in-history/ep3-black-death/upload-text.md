The real history behind the story:
• The Black Death reached Messina, Sicily, in October 1347 on Genoese galleys from the east. The friar Michael of Piazza wrote that the city drove the ships out of the harbour, too late.
• Between 1347 and 1351 it killed an estimated one third to one half of Europe's population.
• It was caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, identified by Alexandre Yersin in 1894. Its DNA has since been recovered from the teeth of Black Death victims in plague pits.
• It spread through fleas carried by rats; many researchers now think human fleas and body lice also played a big part.
• In 1348 the medical faculty of Paris blamed "corrupted air" and a conjunction of the planets.
• In 1377 the city of Ragusa (Dubrovnik) passed the first quarantine law: 30 days of isolation (trentino) for arrivals. It was later extended to 40 days (quaranta giorni), giving us the word "quarantine".
• Rat guards (discs on mooring ropes) are still used on ships today.
• The famous beaked plague-doctor masks date from the 1600s, about 300 years after the Black Death.
• Plague still exists today and is curable with antibiotics if treated early.
Tommaso, Lorenzo, Bianca, Master Galeno and the story are made up. Health tips: wash your hands with soap for 20 seconds; stay home when sick; see a doctor early.
