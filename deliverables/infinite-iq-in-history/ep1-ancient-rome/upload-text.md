The real history behind the story:
• Roman fullers (launderers) really cleaned cloth with stale urine, which turns into ammonia. Pee was collected from public pots.
• Rome's apartment blocks (insulae) were made largely of wood, with the cheapest, most dangerous homes on the top floors.
• Greek engineers such as Ctesibius of Alexandria built piston pumps centuries before 64 AD.
• Rome's fire brigade, the vigiles (about 7,000 men in seven cohorts), was created by Augustus and used buckets, pumps, axes and blankets soaked in water.
• The Great Fire began on the night of 18–19 July 64 AD in shops by the Circus Maximus and burned for six days. Of Rome's 14 districts, 3 were destroyed and 7 badly damaged (Tacitus, Annals 15.38–41).
• It was finally stopped at the foot of the Esquiline Hill by demolishing buildings to create a firebreak (Tacitus, Annals 15.40).
• Afterwards Nero ordered wider streets, height limits, stone construction and water kept ready to fight fires (Tacitus, Annals 15.43).
• Nero didn't fiddle while Rome burned; fiddles didn't exist. Tacitus says he was at Antium and opened his gardens to the homeless.
Felix, Cassius, Aemilius and the story are made up. Fire-safety tips: stay low under smoke, close doors behind you, know two ways out.
