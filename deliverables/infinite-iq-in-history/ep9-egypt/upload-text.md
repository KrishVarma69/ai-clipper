The real history behind the story:
• The Great Pyramid of Giza was built for King Khufu around 2560 BC and stayed the tallest human-made structure for roughly 3,800 years.
• It contains more than two million stone blocks; the builders had no iron tools, no pulleys and didn't use wheels for construction.
• The workers were paid labourers, not slaves. They lived in a town beside the pyramid with bakeries and breweries, and work gangs scratched names such as "Friends of Khufu" into the stones.
• In 2014, physicists at the University of Amsterdam showed that wetting sand to the right degree roughly halves the force needed to pull a sledge. A painting in the tomb of Djehutihotep shows a man pouring water in front of a sledge.
• The Diary of Merer (the Wadi al-Jarf papyri, found in 2013) records boats carrying limestone for the pyramid; it's the oldest known inscribed papyrus.
• The pyramid is aligned to true north within about a tenth of a degree, and its base is level to within about two centimetres. Exactly how this was done is still debated; star-sighting and water-levelling are leading theories.
• Skeletons from the workers' cemetery show healed fractures, evidence of medical care.
Ipi, Senu and the story are made up. Tips: drink before you're thirsty in the heat; lift with your legs; stop the work if someone is hurt.
