The real history behind the story:
• Shackleton's Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition left South Georgia in December 1914 aboard Endurance, planning to cross Antarctica on foot.
• Endurance was trapped in Weddell Sea ice in January 1915, crushed, and sank on 21 November 1915. The 28 men camped on drifting ice for months.
• A stowaway, Perce Blackborow, hid aboard in Buenos Aires. Shackleton reportedly told him that stowaways would be eaten first if food ran out. He survived.
• The crew built ice kennels for the dogs, nicknamed "dogloos".
• Fresh seal and penguin meat provided vitamin C; scurvy did not take hold on the ice.
• In April 1916 the men sailed three lifeboats to Elephant Island, their first land in 497 days.
• Shackleton and five men sailed the 22.5-foot James Caird about 800 miles to South Georgia in roughly 16 days. Navigator Frank Worsley fixed their position from only a handful of sun sightings.
• Landing on the wrong side of the island, they made the first crossing of its mountains in about 36 hours, at one point sliding down a slope on a coiled rope, and reached the Stromness whaling station.
• All 28 men survived; the Elephant Island party was rescued on 30 August 1916.
• The wreck of Endurance was found in 2022 at about 3,000 m depth, remarkably intact and upright.
