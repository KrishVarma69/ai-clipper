The real history behind the story:
• The Battle of Thermopylae was fought in 480 BC at a narrow pass between mountains and the sea, near hot springs (the name means "Hot Gates").
• King Leonidas and 300 Spartans, with a few thousand other Greeks, held the pass against the army of Xerxes for three days. Ancient writers claimed millions of Persians; modern estimates are far lower, often around 100,000–200,000.
• Herodotus records the Spartan Dienekes answering that if the arrows hid the sun, "we shall fight in the shade".
• A local man, Ephialtes, showed the Persians a mountain path around the pass. Herodotus says the Greek guards first heard them coming through the rustling of fallen oak leaves.
• On the last day, Leonidas sent most of the Greek army away; the Spartans and about a thousand others stayed and fought to the end.
• The Greek fleet at Artemisium withdrew after news of the pass reached it, and defeated the Persian navy at Salamis weeks later.
• Simonides' epitaph for the Spartans: "Go tell the Spartans, stranger passing by, that here, obedient to their laws, we lie."
