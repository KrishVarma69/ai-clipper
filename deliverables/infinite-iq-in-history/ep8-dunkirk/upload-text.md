The real history behind the story:
• Operation Dynamo, the evacuation of Allied soldiers from Dunkirk, ran from 26 May to 4 June 1940.
• Planners initially hoped to rescue around 45,000 men. More than 338,000 British and French soldiers were evacuated.
• The beaches shelve so gently that large ships couldn't get close, so hundreds of civilian "little ships" crossed the Channel to ferry men out. Many still exist today.
• Ships used three routes: Z (about 39 miles, but under German guns), X (about 55 miles, through minefields) and Y (about 87 miles).
• Smoke from burning oil tanks hung over the beaches.
• Soldiers built improvised jetties by driving lorries into the sea and laying planks across them.
• The East Mole, a long breakwater never meant for ships, became the main embarkation point; most of the men rescued left along it.
• Dunkirk's tidal range is around five metres, which shaped when boats could reach the beach.
