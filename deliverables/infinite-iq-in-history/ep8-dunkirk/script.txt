You have infinite IQ. And you just woke up in a boat. A small wooden motor boat, tied up in Ramsgate harbour, England. May, nineteen forty. Somebody is poking you with an oar. Oi! This is my grandad's boat, not a hotel! This is Alfie. Fourteen. Fisherman's grandson. He can tie forty kinds of knot and has never once lost a fight with a seagull. In the next week, you'll turn a row of army lorries into a pier. You'll get thrown off a yacht by a boy in a blazer. And under the bombs, on the last night, he'll have to choose between going home... and one more trip. So what if you had infinite IQ at Dunkirk?
First, the facts in your head. Across the Channel, the British army is trapped. More than three hundred thousand soldiers, British and French, squeezed onto the beaches around a French town called Dunkirk. The German army is closing in from every side. The plan to rescue them is called Operation Dynamo. The people in charge hope to save about forty five thousand men. That's it. One in seven.
And there's a problem nobody can fix. The beaches are long and flat. The sea stays shallow for hundreds of metres. The big navy ships need deep water. They can't get close. So the soldiers wade out up to their necks... and wait.
Which is why the navy is asking for help. Anyone with a boat. Fishing boats. Ferries. Pleasure yachts. Lifeboats. Hundreds of little ships, crossing the Channel, to carry soldiers from the beach out to the big ships.
Then you meet Harry. Seventeen. School blazer. His father owns the biggest motor yacht in the harbour, and Harry is taking her across. His father gave him one order. Bring her home without a scratch. You can come, I suppose. Don't touch anything shiny.
The crossing. There are three routes across. The shortest is right under the German guns. The longest is nearly ninety miles. You pick the middle route, through the gaps in the minefields, and keep your eyes on the water. Alfie spots a mine first. That's not a seagull!
Dunkirk. The sky is black. A giant oil refinery is on fire, and the smoke hangs over the whole beach, like a curtain. You realise that's good. Bombers can't hit what they can't see.
And the beach. Long lines of soldiers, snaking into the sea. Each one waiting for a boat. You do the maths. At this speed, it'll take weeks. They don't have weeks.
So Harry makes it official. He doesn't want sand on his father's carpets. You're put ashore to organise the queues. This is the most dangerous place in France. It's perfect. Because from here, you can see the two things nobody's using properly.
Number one... the lorries. Hundreds of army trucks, abandoned on the sand. At low tide, you get the soldiers to drive them into the sea, in a long line, side by side. Then lash planks across their roofs. And when the tide comes in... it's a pier. Little boats can tie up at the end. That's real. Soldiers really built lorry jetties at Dunkirk.
Number two... the Mole. A long, thin concrete wall, sticking out into the sea, built to protect the harbour from waves. Not built for ships at all. But on one side, the water is deep. A navy captain had the same idea you did. Big ships started tying up alongside it, and soldiers walked along the top, straight on board. Most of the soldiers saved at Dunkirk walked down that wall.
Alfie is amazed. So the sea goes up and down... and you just wait for it? The tide at Dunkirk rises and falls by about five metres. Time it right, and your boat floats right up to the soldiers. Time it wrong, and you're stuck on the sand till tomorrow.
The bombs. Planes dive out of the smoke. Everyone throws themselves flat. And you notice something strange. The bombs are landing in soft sand. The sand swallows most of the blast. Lie flat, in the dunes, and you have a chance. Out on the hard road, you don't.
Day after day, the little ships go back and forth. Harry's yacht carries soldiers out to the destroyers, twenty at a time. The white paint is gone. The brass is dented. There's a hole in the side, stuffed with an army coat.
June the third. The last night. The Germans are almost at the beach. Every boat is being told to go home. Harry's yacht is leaking. His father's order is still ringing in his ears. Bring her home without a scratch. Too late for that. But there are still men standing in the water. Hundreds of them. If he goes back now, the yacht might sink. If he goes home... they don't. Home... or one more trip.
Harry looks at the beach. Then he turns the yacht around. He makes three more trips before dawn. Alfie bails water with a saucepan the whole way home. Fifty two soldiers squeezed onto a yacht built for eight.
Back in Ramsgate, Harry's father is waiting on the quay. He looks at the dents, the hole, the missing paint... and then at the soldiers climbing off. He doesn't say a word about the scratches. He just shakes his son's hand.
So, is any of this real? Yes. The evacuation of Dunkirk, Operation Dynamo, ran from the end of May to the fourth of June, nineteen forty. The planners hoped to save around forty five thousand men. In the end, more than three hundred and thirty eight thousand soldiers were rescued. Hundreds of civilian boats, the little ships, really crossed the Channel to help. Soldiers really built piers out of lorries, and most men really were taken off along the long concrete Mole. The burning oil really hung over the beaches. Alfie and Harry are made up for the story. Many of the real little ships still exist, and some still sail.
Three things, in case it's ever you. If you're caught in a crowd, stay calm and move with it, not against it. In the water, a life jacket matters more than how well you swim. And in any emergency, follow the people who are organising, or become one. Even geniuses forget.
