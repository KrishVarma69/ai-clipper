The real history behind the story:
• Mount Vesuvius erupted in 79 AD and buried Pompeii and Herculaneum. Before the eruption it was green and covered in vineyards, and most people didn't know it was a volcano.
• Pliny the Younger, about 17, watched from Misenum across the bay. In letters to the historian Tacitus (Letters 6.16 and 6.20) he described the cloud as shaped like an umbrella pine, earth tremors in the days before, and people tying pillows on their heads against falling stones. Volcanologists still call this type of eruption "Plinian".
• His uncle, Pliny the Elder, admiral of the fleet at Misenum, sailed across the bay to rescue people, was held at Stabiae by the wind and died there.
• Pumice fell on Pompeii for roughly 18 hours, collapsing roofs. The deadly pyroclastic surges arrived the next morning.
• The date was long given as 24 August, but a charcoal inscription found in 2018 suggests the eruption happened in October.
• Around 1,150 bodies have been found at Pompeii. Most of the town's roughly 11,000 people probably escaped.
• Election slogans were painted all over Pompeii's walls. Garum, the fermented fish sauce, was one of the town's famous products.
• Vesuvius is still active; around 700,000 people live in its official danger zone.
Rufus, Livia and the story are made up. Safety tips: drop, cover and hold on in an earthquake; evacuate right away when told; keep a go-bag by the door.
